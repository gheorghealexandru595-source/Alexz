In this town, every hour on your phone makes your eyes bigger. That's why gamers have eyes like plates, and grandmas have tiny little dots. My eyes were normal. But my best friend Noah had eyes the size of pizzas. He hadn't seen a sunset in a year. Meanwhile, Lily didn't own a phone. She read books. She climbed trees. She knew everyone's name. Then I got a new game, and played all night. My eyes ballooned. Mom screamed. And that's when I had an idea. If I ditched my phone for a day, they'd shrink back. I hid it in a drawer. I checked it anyway. Still huge. I tried one quick video. Even bigger. Then Lily needed help with her treehouse, and I forgot my phone existed. My eyes shrank right back. So I did real stuff. I played soccer. I baked cookies. I hugged my mom. By dinner, my eyes were normal. But then I saw Noah, alone, glued to his phone. I'd spent all day outside while my best friend was stuck in a screen. So I sat down. Look up. Why? I pointed at the sky. His eyes shrank a little. Then he put his phone away. For the first time in a year, Noah watched a sunset. Then his phone buzzed. We both looked. Eh. It can wait.
