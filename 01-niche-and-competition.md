# 01 — Niche and competition

The method comes from Vol. 1, ch. 2 (prompt 2.1). Each niche is scored 1 to 5 on seven criteria:
a) proven demand · b) how much the problem hurts · c) demos on a phone screen in 3 seconds · d) a great product can be built with AI in under a week · e) low policy risk · f) room for a second and third product · g) fits a likeable AI creator.

> The scores are my estimate, based on the sources in the README. Demand is confirmed by similar products selling on Etsy, Payhip and Gumroad, but I don't have official sales figures from Etsy.

## Top 3, briefly

### 1. Budgeting for irregular income (the winner)
- **Who buys:** Americans and Brits aged 22–40 who are paid a different amount every week: servers and bartenders (tips), nurses and shift workers, delivery and rideshare drivers, freelancers, people paid on commission.
- **The pain, in their words:** "Every budget app assumes I get the same paycheck." · "Good week, I overspend. Bad week, I panic." · "I never know how much I can actually spend." · "Rent week is always a nightmare." · "I forgot to save for taxes again."
- **Competitors and their weak spots:**
  - *Irregular Income Baseline Planner* (Payhip, $5): only calculates the lowest month and doesn't tell you what to do with the money week by week.
  - *Freelance Budget Pro* (Gumroad, $29): built in Notion, complicated, made for freelancers with a business, not for servers.
  - Free templates (Taskade, Jotform): generic, no guidance.
  - Generic budgets on Etsy: they all assume a fixed salary. The 2026 sources say clearly that generic planners are saturated and that niche ones sell, "by income level" or "for irregular income".
- **Our angle:** one Google Sheet where you **type what you earned this week and it tells you exactly how much you can spend, how much goes aside for rent and how much for taxes**. Built for people paid weekly or daily, not only for freelancers with a business.
- **Main risk:** tax advice. The product only has a "tax jar" with a percentage the user chooses, no tax advice.

### 2. General budgeting + no-spend challenge
Very high demand, but huge competition and identical products. It works as a **small second product** in the product ladder (see `02`).

### 3. Meal planning on a budget
Looks good on screen, but harder to stand out and needs tested recipes. It can be the second niche, after 30 steady sales (Vol. 2, ch. 10).

## The decision (double-checked)

I chose **Budgeting for irregular income**. It has the most painful problem in the budgeting list, a clear audience that generic products don't serve, a 3-second demo ("I type $412 and the sheet tells me what I can spend") and a natural product ladder. First and second place are close; what would change my choice is weak validation on TikTok in the first 14 days (under 65% of viewers staying past 3 seconds on every video), in which case you switch to general budgeting with the same character.

## NICHE BRIEF (paste it into any new prompt)

```
NICHE BRIEF
Niche: budgeting for people with irregular, weekly or tip-based income (US + UK).
Buyer: 22-40, servers, bartenders, nurses on shifts, delivery and rideshare drivers,
freelancers, commission sales. Paid different amounts every week or day.
Main pain (their words): "Every budget app assumes I get the same paycheck."
"Good week I overspend, bad week I panic." "Rent week is always a nightmare."
Core product: The Uneven Paycheck Kit, Google Sheets, $17 (launch $14).
Type this week's income, it tells you: safe-to-spend, rent/bills set-aside, savings, tax jar.
Free lead magnet: Lowest-Week Calculator (1 tab) – find the number you can always count on.
Follow-ups: 30-Day No-Spend Reset for Uneven Pay ($9); Bundle with tax-jar tracker
and side-gig tracker ($39).
Creator persona: Nora, openly AI creator, ~28, cozy apartment, warm big-sister vibe,
General American accent, calm and funny, never preachy.
Content pillars: 1) budget your uneven pay, 2) rent-week and bill-week rescue,
3) small money habits for shift and tip workers.
Hooks: "If your paycheck is different every week, normal budgets will fail you."
"Servers, this is how you budget tips." "Stop budgeting from your best week."
"The number you should budget from is not your average." "Rent week, but calm."
Avoid: investing, credit repair, debt promises, income claims, tax advice beyond
"set money aside", real brands or apps, fake testimonials.
```
