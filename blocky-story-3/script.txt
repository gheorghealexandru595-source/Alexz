In this town, every excuse you make makes you heavier. That's why lazy kids are stuck waist-deep, and honest people practically bounce. I was only ankle-deep. But my best friend Leo was chest-deep. He hadn't sat in a chair since fourth grade. Meanwhile, Zara never made excuses. Late for class? My fault. Forgot homework? My fault. Broke a window? Also my fault. Then I forgot my homework. My dog ate it. Thud. I sank to my knees. Zara winced. And that's when I had an idea. If I owned up to everything, I'd be light as Zara. I said sorry to a plant. Nothing. I apologized for a sneeze. Still nothing. Then I knocked over a kid's tray, and said, sorry, my fault. Pop. I shot out of the floor. So I owned everything. My messy locker. My bad haircut. My singing. By lunch, I bounced like Zara. But then I saw Leo, chin-deep and alone. I'd spent all day bouncing while my best friend was sinking. I knelt down. What's wrong? It's never my fault. Just try once. He took a deep breath. I'm scared I'm not good enough. He shot up to his waist. Also, I broke your pencil. For the first time since fourth grade, Leo sat in a chair. Plus, that sneeze was mine. I stared at him. Totally worth it.
