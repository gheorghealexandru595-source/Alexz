In this town, every lie you tell makes your shadow a little longer. My name's Leo, and I thought I was a pretty honest kid. Then on Monday, my mom asked if I'd brushed my teeth. I said, yes. My shadow grew one step. Nobody noticed. On Tuesday, I told my teacher the dog ate my homework. We don't have a dog. My shadow reached the door. By Wednesday, I was lying about everything. I said I liked my aunt's soup. I said I'd already cleaned my room. I said I scored a goal. By Thursday, my shadow was so long it went down the whole street. It tripped the mailman. It scared the neighbor's cat. And at night, it lay across the whole town like a black river. People started asking questions. Leo, why is your shadow in the bakery? I said, that's not my shadow. It grew another block. Then came the worst part. My little sister Mia got lost at the night market. Everyone was looking for her. And I couldn't find her, because my own shadow covered every street. Everything was dark. So I stood in the middle of the square, and I told the truth. Mom, I never brushed my teeth. Miss Rose, I don't have a dog. Aunt Nora, your soup is terrible. With every truth, my shadow shrank back. Street by street, the lights came back. And there, under a lamp, sat Mia, eating cotton candy. She said, I wasn't lost. I lied so you'd come find me. And right behind her, her shadow was just starting to grow.
