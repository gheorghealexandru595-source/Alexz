In this town, every friend you make lights a lantern above your head. That's why popular kids glow like birthday cakes, and lonely kids walk home in the dark. I had about three lanterns. But my best friend Finn didn't have a single one. His was the only dark house on the street. He'd never had a birthday party, because nobody could find his house. Meanwhile, Coco had ninety-nine lanterns. She lit up the whole block. Moths followed her home. Planes waved at her. Then Jake fell off his bike, and I laughed at him. Pop. One of my lanterns went out. Jake didn't even look at me. And that's when I had an idea. If I made a hundred friends, I'd beat Coco. I handed out flyers. Nothing. I offered free candy. Still nothing. Then I helped a new kid find his classroom. We talked all through lunch. Ding. A new lantern lit up. So I made friends everywhere. The chess club. The skaters. The lunch lady. By the weekend, I had twenty lanterns. But then I walked past Finn's house. It was still completely dark. Finn was sitting on his steps, alone. I'd spent all week collecting friends while forgetting my oldest one. So I knocked on his door with a party hat. He asked, what's this? Your birthday party. You never had one. Then I invited everyone. One by one, they showed up. And one by one, his lanterns lit up. By midnight, Finn's house was the brightest on the street. Even Coco came. She squinted and said, I need sunglasses. Best party in town.
