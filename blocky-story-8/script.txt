In this town, every thank-you you say makes you glow. That's why polite people light up the street, and rude people walk around in the dark. I had a nice warm glow. But my best friend Eli was so dark people bumped into him. He hadn't shown up in a class photo in two years. Meanwhile, Rosa thanked everything. The bus driver? Thanks! Her sandwich? Thanks! A pigeon? Thanks! Then my grandma knit me a sweater, and I just said, it's itchy. I went dim. Grandma squinted to find me. And that's when I had an idea. If I thanked everyone, I'd glow like Rosa. I thanked a wall. Nothing. I yelled thank you really loud. Still nothing. Then the janitor fixed my locker, and I really meant it. Thank you. I lit right up. So I thanked everyone. The lunch lady. The crossing guard. My little sister. By lunch, I was glowing like Rosa. But then I bumped into Eli, sitting in the dark, alone. I'd spent all day glowing while my best friend was fading out. So I gave him half my sandwich. He asked, why? Because you're my best friend. He smiled. Thank you. No, really. Thank you. For the first time in two years, Eli showed up in the class photo. Then he said, the sandwich was gross though. I stared at him. Then I said, thanks for eating it.
